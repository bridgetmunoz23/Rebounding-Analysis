The Value of Every Loose Ball

A possession-by-possession investigation into how GW's crash decisions and turnovers impacted the final score against Georgetown. This is measured by an "opportunity cost" of missing a rebound or turnover, or points Georgetown scores on the immediate possession when GW fails to gain or keep possession.

Bridget Muñoz · George Washington University Men's Basketball · Sample: George Washington versus Georgetown scrimmage (73–64 loss) against 24-25 nonconference data
Key findings
  • With three offensive crashers, GW converted 80% of misses into rebounds at 1.25 PPP, however, the most efficient look was also the rarest (5 FREQ).
  • GW's most frequent number of rebounders (2 crashers) resulted in 1.29 opponent PPP when it failed.
  • Cost of turnovers dropped almost to zero after halftime (1.375 → 0.125 pts/TO) despite similar frequency (7 in H1 vs 8 in H2).

Section 3

Defensive rebounding inefficiency with 2 crashers

Section 4

Comparing turnovers in half one versus half two

More turnovers were committed in half two than half one (8 vs 7), but opponent conversion fell from 1.375 → 0.125 pts/TO. If Georgetown had continued successfully scoring off turnovers in the second half at the H1 rate, this would have meant roughly 10 more points in the final-score difference*.

*8 H2 TOs × 1.375 ≈ 11 projected pts vs. the 1 actually scored.

Comparison to Temple Scrimmage

In GW's scrimmage against Temple University, GW had 16 TO (almost identical), but Temple scored with 1.52 pts/TO (almost double). Cost-per-turnover is not just dependent on how GW cares for the ball, but how our opponent uses their opportunities.

Methodology & Limitations

Building the Study
  • Possession-by-possession tracking from game film.
  • Custom variables: IP (in-paint but not crashing), p-OREB% (per-player conversion rate on rebound attempts).
  • Single-game sample for 25-26, but the same framework was applied across the full 24-25 non-conference season for validation.
  • Opponent PPP is sensitive to opponent transition strength.
OREB% / DREB%: rebounds won ÷ rebound opportunities PPP: points per possession (own team) Opp. PPP: points scored by opponent on the resulting possession Crasher: player who actively pursued the rebound
