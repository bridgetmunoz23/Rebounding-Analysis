The Value of Every Loose Ball

A possession-by-possession investigation into how GW's crash decisions and turnovers impacted the final score across five games. This is measured by an "opportunity cost" of missing a rebound or turnover, or points the opponent scores on the immediate possession when GW fails to gain or keep possession.

Bridget Muñoz · George Washington University Men's Basketball · Sample: 5-game summary (Maine, USF, GMU, Murray State, McNeese) compared to games 1-8 of the nonconference 24-25 season. Rebounds (25-26): n=271. Turnovers (25-26): n=69.
Key findings
  • One GW player in position who does not go for the ball drops defensive rebounding from 86% to 25%.
  • With everyone boxing out and nobody free to chase, GW gets the defensive board 37% of the time. One player breaking off to actually go get it raises it to 85%.
  • Designated crashers convert no better than everyone else (24.5% vs 23.4%). The role produces effort, not finishing.

Section 6

Designated rebounders vs other players

GW's designated crashers are Player C, Player F, and Player I. Designated players do not seem to be more effective rebounders. The designated three convert 24.5% of their crash attempts, other players convert 23.4%. The role provides more frequency and effort in the paint, but not rebounding percentage.

Designated rebounders vs other players

OREB% on crash attempts (designated 61/249, other players 90/384). IP rate on rebound appearances (designated 33/279, other players 79/461). Lower IP is better.

Methodology & Limitations

Building the study
  • Points off a failed rebound count opponent points only on possessions where GW did not secure the board (REB = 0).
  • Opponent PPP is sensitive to the opponent's transition strength and varies game to game.
  • Designated, nail, and turnover-player figures were parsed from hand-logged jersey cells now being standardized, so treat those as solid but not final. Rebound rates, IP, and crasher-count tables are clean.
  • This is a five-game sample. The single-game limitation from the earlier study no longer applies, but five games is still a modest sample at the per-level breakdowns.
Designated crasher: player assigned the crashing role on a possession (Player C, Player F, Player I) IP: in position but made no effort to rebound Free chaser: crashers minus players boxing out, the bodies free to go get the ball REB / OREB% / DREB%: rebounds won divided by rebound opportunities
